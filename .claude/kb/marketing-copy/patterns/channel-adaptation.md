# Pattern: Channel Adaptation

> Reusing a case study's hook on another channel (LinkedIn, email, WhatsApp) requires adapting
> format and length — never just pasting the page copy.

---

## Per-Channel Shape

| Channel | Length | Structure | Notes |
|---------|--------|-----------|-------|
| LinkedIn post | 3-6 short paragraphs | Hook line → problem → what was built → result → link | Line breaks between every 1-2 sentences; native platform convention |
| Email | Subject + short body | Subject names the outcome, body is 3-5 sentences + link | Subject line is its own headline — apply `concepts/headline-formulas.md` to it separately from the body |
| WhatsApp / DM | 2-4 sentences | Direct, conversational, no headline framing | Reads like a message from a person, not a forwarded ad |
| Twitter/X | 1-3 short posts (thread optional) | Hook → 1-2 supporting facts → link | Compress hardest; only the single strongest fact survives |

---

## What Must Survive Every Adaptation

The **hook** — the specific transformation the case proves — must appear in the first sentence or
line on every channel, regardless of length constraints. If the adaptation buries the hook to fit a
format, it's not adapted correctly; it's just cut.

---

## What Changes Freely

- Tone formality (LinkedIn slightly more composed than WhatsApp)
- Which supporting detail is kept vs. cut
- Whether the CTA links to the case page or straight to contact

---

## Never Do

- Copy-paste the case page's Problem/Approach/Solution/Result headers into a post — those are page
  navigation aids, not readable social copy
- Add a stat or claim in the adapted version that isn't already on the source case page — the case
  page is the source of truth; adaptations only compress it, never extend it
