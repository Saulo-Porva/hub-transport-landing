# Positioning Statement

> Positioning answers one question before any copy gets written: when someone reads this, who do
> they understand you to be, relative to their alternatives?

---

## The Studio vs. Agency Choice (already decided for this project)

This project positions as a **founder-led applied studio**, not a generic software house. That
decision has concrete copy consequences:

| Studio positioning says | Generic agency positioning would say |
|--------------------------|----------------------------------------|
| "I built this after someone described the exact problem." | "We deliver end-to-end solutions for enterprises." |
| Named, specific case studies as the entire proof | Client logos, team size, process diagrams as proof |
| First person throughout | "We" / "our team" throughout |
| Small scope framed as attention and speed | Small scope hidden or apologized for |

Any new copy — a new case study, a new section, an outreach message — should be checked against
this table before writing. Drifting toward the agency column, even in one section, weakens the
whole site's credibility because visitors notice inconsistency in voice faster than they notice
any single claim.

---

## Writing a Positioning Line for a New Case

Template: *"{Client type} had {specific recognizable problem}. I built {what it does, plainly}."*

Not: *"We provide innovative solutions for {industry}."*

---

## Segmenting Without Sounding Corporate

The site serves several very different buyer types (fleet managers, clinic owners, real estate
agencies, gym trainers...). Resist the urge to write one "universal" positioning line that tries to
speak to all of them — it ends up specific to none. Each case study's positioning is local to that
page and that reader; the home page's job is to prove range exists, not to summarize every reader's
pain in one sentence.
