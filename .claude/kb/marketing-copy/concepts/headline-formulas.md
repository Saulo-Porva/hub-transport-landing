# Headline Formulas

> A headline's only job is to make the right reader keep reading. It doesn't need to be clever —
> it needs to name a transformation that reader recognizes.

---

## Formula 1 — Before → After

```
{Problem state} → {solved state}
```
Example: "TruckPilot — Fleet Compliance on WhatsApp" implies paperwork-bound compliance became a
WhatsApp conversation. Works best when the "before" is instantly recognizable to the target reader.

## Formula 2 — Named Object, Reframed

```
{Product/thing} — {what it actually is, stated plainly}
```
Example: "A delivery harness, not another chat window." Names what it's NOT to cut through a
crowded category (every AI tool claims to be an "assistant").

## Formula 3 — Outcome-First

```
{Concrete outcome}, without {the usual cost}
```
Example: "Compliant rosters without the spreadsheet marathon." Leads with the result, names the
pain being removed.

## Formula 4 — Direct Statement (personal/studio positioning)

```
I {verb} {specific thing} {for/that matters to} {audience}
```
Example: "I build the software your business is still missing." Works for hero-level, identity
copy — not for individual case studies, which should lead with the product/outcome instead.

---

## What Breaks a Headline

- Abstract nouns doing the work instead of concrete ones ("efficiency", "excellence", "innovation")
- Claiming the transformation without naming either side of it ("Better fleet management")
- Borrowing a big-company register the founder can't back up ("The #1 platform for...")
- A headline that would fit any product in the category — if it could sit on a competitor's page
  unchanged, it hasn't earned its specificity yet

---

## Case-Study Headline vs. Hero Headline

Hero headline (home page) sells the founder/studio — Formula 4 territory.
Case-study headline sells the specific product/outcome — Formula 1, 2, or 3. Never reuse hero-level
identity language ("I build...") as a case-study headline; it dilutes the specific proof that page
exists to deliver.
