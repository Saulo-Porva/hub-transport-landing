# Social Proof

> Social proof is the fastest trust-builder in copy — and the fastest trust-destroyer when it turns
> out to be soft. For a small, founder-led studio, this trade-off is sharper than for a large
> company: there's no brand reputation to absorb the damage of one caught exaggeration.

---

## The Verifiability Test

Before any number, result, or claim ships, it must pass:

**Can the founder point to exactly where this came from, right now, without hedging?**

If the answer requires "well, roughly..." or "in most cases..." — the claim needs to be softened to
match what's actually known, or removed until it's confirmed.

---

## Real Screenshots vs. Illustrated Mockups

Both are legitimate social proof, but they carry different weight and different rules:

| Type | When to use | Rule |
|------|-------------|------|
| Real screenshot | Product is live and the specific screen was confirmed usable for publishing | Must not expose real client names, real client branding not cleared for use, or any personal data of a third party — anonymize in the DOM before capture, not after |
| Illustrated mockup | No live/reachable deployment, or the real screen can't be cleared for privacy | Must visually signal it's illustrative (same style as the product's other case-study art), and copy must never claim it's a live screenshot |

Never present a mockup as if it were a captured screenshot — that's exactly the kind of claim that
fails the verifiability test if anyone ever asks.

---

## Numbers: Real, Rounded, or Removed

- **Real**: "Invoicing: 60 days → same month" — confirmed by the founder, keep exact.
- **Rounded**: only round in the direction that's still true (60 days is already round; don't
  further round a precise confirmed number in a way that could overstate it).
- **Removed**: if no real number exists yet for a case, don't invent a placeholder one "to fill the
  space" — use a qualitative result statement instead (see `concepts/benefit-vs-feature.md`).

---

## Testimonials and Client Names

Do not fabricate a testimonial, quote, or named client attribution under any circumstance, even as
a "placeholder to replace later" — a placeholder testimonial left in by accident is a real quote
falsely attributed once it's live. If there's no real testimonial yet, the page simply doesn't have
one.
