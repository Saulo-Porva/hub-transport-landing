Workforce — Compliant Rosters, No Spreadsheet Marathon

A system that reads clocked hours and builds the schedule, instead of a manager rebuilding it from scratch every week.

Client

Multi-shift operations

Sector

Retail & Workforce Ops

Stack

Scheduling engine + labor-law rules

Status

Live

Overtime, cut

Fewer excess hours, fewer compliance flags

01 / The Problem

Shift planning was a weekly spreadsheet exercise — built from memory, adjusted by hand, and only checked against labor law after the fact, when an overtime bill or a compliance flag showed the mistake. One wrong roster was one violation away, and nobody had time to check every shift against every rule before publishing.

02 / Before

  • ✕Rosters rebuilt from memory in a spreadsheet, every single week
  • ✕Labor-law rules checked after publishing, not before
  • ✕Overtime discovered on the payroll bill, not on the roster
  • ✕No single source of truth for who actually clocked what

03 / The System

I started from the constraint that actually matters: what the law requires — rest periods, weekly hour caps, contract terms — has to be satisfied before a schedule is even suggested, not checked afterward. So the engine reads clocked hours first, applies the rules as hard constraints, and only then proposes the shift mix, instead of leaving compliance as a manual review step at the end.
